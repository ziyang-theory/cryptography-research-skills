# Mathematical exposition

Use when drafting or revising the presentation of definitions, notation, equations, lemmas, or a supplied proof. Apply the relevant diagnostic locally. Preserve the claim, quantifiers, hypotheses, dependencies, macros, and authorial choices. Exposition checks do not establish proof validity; leave missing arguments visible unless completing them is also requested.

## Give formulas a role in the argument

Introduce a substantial calculation by explaining what it will establish, then identify the consequential inference or hypothesis used. Integrate formulas into grammatical sentences, with punctuation appropriate to the sentence. Do not make readers infer the connection between successive displays from their algebra alone.

As a diagnostic, skim past complicated formulas: can the prose still convey the argument's direction and the purpose of its main steps? This does not require repeating every equation in words or making the prose a substitute for the mathematics. Add explanation where it reveals a dependency, choice, or conclusion; omit routine algebra when the intended reader can recover it reliably.

## Explain consequential definitional choices

Alongside an unfamiliar definition, explain the distinction it captures and why its unusual clauses matter. Where the source supports the distinction, separate a notational convenience from a restriction needed for a theorem. Do not invent a necessity claim merely to motivate a condition.

A complementary explanation or representative example should help readers interpret the formal statement. Keep the formal definition authoritative, including qualifications that an example does not exercise. If a clause's purpose is unclear, flag that uncertainty instead of simplifying it away.

## Keep notation learnable and scoped

Introduce symbols when their role becomes clear. Keep temporary notation within its proof or section, and avoid importing proof machinery into a theorem statement when readers need only its hypotheses and conclusion. Retain indices and parameters when they expose meaningful dependence; shortening notation must not suggest independence or uniformity absent from the claim.

Use stable names for recurring objects and distinguish objects that behave differently. During local edits, preserve existing macros and conventions rather than renaming throughout the manuscript. For an authorized larger revision, weigh the readability gain against the burden of relearning notation and updating its consumers.

## Separate reusable conclusions from temporary work

A lemma can tell readers what they need to retain while allowing them to leave its internal calculations and notation behind. Extract one when the conclusion is reused, marks a meaningful conceptual step, or simplifies the surrounding argument. Avoid turning every short calculation into a numbered result.

Make the lemma's role apparent before or after its statement. Choose detail according to the intended reader and the difficulty of the inference: explain an unfamiliar or consequential step even when its calculation is short. Preserve an existing compact proof when its dependencies are already clear.

## Make imported results usable

For a central or unfamiliar cited result, state the version actually needed in local notation, cite its exact source locator, and explain how the current objects satisfy its hypotheses. Keep distinctions between the original theorem, an immediate specialization, and a new corollary requiring argument.

Translate notation without dropping premises, changing quantifier order, or strengthening conclusions. If the source or applicability has not been checked, leave that dependency visible. A polished restatement cannot verify it; use the literature or proof workflow when that additional work is requested or needed.
